Equitability

  • Accessible design matters because we want to create a society that everyone can participate in and be a part of. We want all people to be able to add their contributions to the world and we do not want anyone to feel limited due to having differing abilities.
  • Accessible design matters because barriers from inaccessible technologies can exclude individuals from opportunities taken for granted by the general public. These opportunities should be available to the entire public.
  • Public institutions and public services should especially have accessible design. Public institutions, companies that receive government money, and the government are required to use accessible designs.

Necessity

Especially since the internet is so heavily relied upon, not having access to it would make it hard for individuals to complete tasks necessary for everyday life.

Independence

Having access to the internet allows individuals with disabilities to have independence and personal autonomy. Individuals should not have to rely on a family member or friend to complete a task on the internet and by making the internet accessible we can eliminate this gap.

Public Benefit

  • Making websites easier to use is a general benefit. Creating accessible designs often makes websites easier for everyone to use.
  • Many of the designs that benefit people with disabilities also benefit you:
    • Video captions: Captions benefit people who are hard of hearing, but they can also help you understand what is happening when watching a movie with talkative friends or watching a movie in another language.
    • High color contrast: High contrast helps people with low vision and can also help you use your phone in bright sunlight.
    • Clear headings: Headings make it easier for screen reader users to navigate pages and make it easier for you to scan a page quickly and understand its main content.
    • Large buttons: Large buttons help people with motor control issues and make it easier for everyone to click buttons.
    • Simple navigation: Understandable, regularly used icons and clear instructions help people with cognitive disabilities navigate websites. They also make unfamiliar websites easier to use and help everyone navigate more quickly.

It Affects YOU

If none of this concerns you and you are still questioning why accessibility matters, take a look at the aging adults in your life. Maybe your parents or grandparents now need glasses and they didn’t used to, or they have trouble working with a small clip because they don’t have the ability to feel textures that you have. Maybe you have to repeat words sometimes or when you show your parents an instagram video they don’t quite understand it and have to watch it a couple of times because the text is small or they can’t quite hear the audio. All of these issues your loved ones are experiencing you will experience at some point too. Don’t you want to make it easier for everyone to access the information so that you can benefit from it one day as well. Remember accessible design is not something that just applies to those around you, or people you do not know but it is something that will apply to you and it might take a while for you to get there, but you will want to have accessible options when you do.

Conclusion

Ultimately it is our job as individuals to make the internet an equitable place that everyone can access. By creating accessible apps we can improve individuals’ access to a world of information and tools including equal access to tools for education, access to entertainment, and access to vital apps like flight booking sites and google search.
