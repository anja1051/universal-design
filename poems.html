Streamers across the sky

I don’t know if you understood how much I loved fireworks,
Or dreaming or writing or scribbling in the margins of my pages,
The pictures were small and uncut,
The words were written in a scribble of might,
But I loved you.
I loved you.
I dreamed of warm sunny days and winters by the fire.
I conjured up pieces from my mind like I was the author of our romance,
Like if I could put together the pieces you would stay.
But you were a moment,
A fragment of a picture that has yet to be completed,
And you were the sun and you shone bright like the world might forget your name if you flickered for even a moment,
And I was caught in your web, in your deep blue eyes from the moment I looked at you.
Sometimes I wish I could come to regret the moments,
Loathe our time together,
Bury the thoughts so deep in the ground that by the time I dig it up it will have turned to gold,
Our love. The treasure.
And it doesn’t matter if I flew too close to the sun,
Or if you burned out into a cold abyss,
Or if we both just turned in different directions,
Looking for the night, searching for the edge of the universe.
The result remains the same,
And I miss your warmth,
I miss you here.
In my life I see flickers of you,
In the fireworks overhead,
The lantern that floats across that silver lake,
But I see new light too.
The cool moon that shines down when you wonder if anyone is even looking,
The flicker of the stars,
The warmth of a home lit with dreams and poetry.
It is peaceful in this world,
And sure it’s heart wrenching and devastating and cold,
But what’s the point of bittersweet without the bite,
Cream without the coffee,
Cold without the blistering heat.
One minute I dance in my living room and the next I sit in silence,
I am simply living what we call life.
And let me fly too close to come falling back down to earth.
Watch me.. I’m smiling

Untitled

Don’t you understand why I follow this pain,
To dredge myself up from death,
To bring the heartbreak into my light.
For when I see this piece of paper,
Don’t I break into tune, and weap,
Gracelessly.
Bury your skeletons in the yard next door,
And watch as mine,
Come Alive!
A spell was cast a long time ago.
On my mind.
And the riots are haunting,
As they partake their golden march upon my mind,
As they scream, out into the cobwebs,
The spiders come to play.
In my castle of silky thrones and wooden nooks,
The kingdom cannot sleep.
For the melody spews from the castle.
Disorderly and deranged,
It seems only ordinary that chaos could follow.
But his mad tune is one for the minds that fail to sleep,
As they look to the wandering stars,
As they fly among the birds,
If I never heard a single word again,
From the mouth I call my home,
And I could never be my own,
Without this pen.

Burnt out Campfires

Do you remember that time,
When fences were optional,
When we had the world at our hands,
And we used it liberally.

Or when we ran through the water,
Ice cold droplets sprayed across our faces,
We were soaked to the bone,
And I could hear your laughter.

And in the summer time,
When we were in the mountains,
We’d hide in the forest,
We’d play kick the can and catch Crawdads with our hands.

Or we’d spend our nights in the col-de-sac,
Screaming in fear,
When that phrase was yelled.
“Ghost In The Graveyard!”

We don’t scream like that anymore,
Or run barefoot down sidewalks,
Climb up trees like monkeys,
Something might brake now.

And the light doesn’t shine the same.
And the clouds don’t make animals.

But I still run through sprinklers,
On summer nights,
Or skinny dip in the lake,
When I think no one’s looking.

And in the summers,
We tell stories around the fire,
That never seems to change.
But now we don’t just listen.

And time moved on,
And we grew up without realizing.
And the light doesn’t shine the same.
But maybe now it shines brighter.

About the Streamers Across the Sky Poem

The poem Streamers Across the Sky uses the loss of a person you love, which I feel is a cumulation of both friendships and relationships ending, and demonstrates the heartbreak that we feel when we lose someone who deeply changes us but at the same time maybe misses pieces of us. This scenario sets up what I really actually find important about this poem, which is the demonstration of the pain of life and how it makes the good times that much better. I love this poem because it gives me a place to recognize the painful moments but shape it into a reason to live. It incapsulates a belief I have about life, which is that life is so much better because of all the hard times and heartbreaks. This is my favorite poem out of the ones I've written. Maybe its because it mentions fireworks. I do love fireworks oh so much.

About the Untitled and Burnt Out Campfires Poems

The untitled poem in the center is a fantasy poem, with allusion to ones mind. It embodies the madness that can come with emotions, the chaos that comes when putting ideas to paper, when spiraling into words, and the almost necessary urge that writing has on an individual when inspiration strikes. I enjoy writing poems with eerie or creepy scenes and themes and I like to compare fantastical things to peoples emotions.

The poem Burnt Out Campfires is an ode to my childhood neighbors, the time we spent together and the memories I've made as I've slowly moved away from my childhood home and childhood friends. Its a bittersweet look that appreciates the many great moments while explaining how the moments have changed. As a kid I used to sit around the campfire and listen to all the "adults" talk and now I sit around the campfire and talk instead of running wild in the neighborhood or campground forests.

Written by Anja Delzell
